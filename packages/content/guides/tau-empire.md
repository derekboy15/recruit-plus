The T'au Empire fights with advanced guns, battlesuits and careful teamwork.

## How this army plays

T'au win the firefight and avoid the brawl. Markerlights mark targets, Commanders and Ethereals buff the shooters, and Fire Warriors, battlesuits and tanks remove whatever you marked.

**Strengths.** Long-ranged, flexible firepower, battlesuits that still shoot when enemies reach them, and plenty of ways to redeploy or arrive from reserves.

**Weaknesses.** Melee is very poor and Fire Warriors are fragile, Toughness 3 infantry. The army relies on combos, so losing a Commander or your markerlight units early hurts.

**What to expect.** You choose Mont'ka (attack early) or Kauyon (strike late) before the game, and that choice shapes your plan. Screen your gunline and fire markerlights first.

## Army rules

**Markerlights.** MARKERLIGHT units can start the Fire Markerlights action at the start of your Movement phase; it completes at the start of your next Shooting phase. For each markerlight, pick an eligible enemy unit within 36" and roll a D6: on a 3+ it gains a Markerlight token. VEHICLE and DRONE units can move while doing this action. When a T'AU EMPIRE unit (but not a T'AU AUXILIARY unit) shoots at a unit with a token, its ranged attacks get +1 to hit. Afterwards, remove one token from each enemy unit it targeted. All tokens are removed at the end of your Shooting phase.

**Tactical Philosophies.** At the start of the battle you commit to one philosophy:

- **Mont'ka** (battle rounds 1 to 3): units can make a Normal Move or Advance and still shoot as if they had Remained Stationary, and re-roll wound rolls of 1 against the closest target within a range that shrinks each round.
- **Kauyon** (battle rounds 3 to 5): units can shoot after Falling Back at -1 to hit, and units that didn't Fall Back score extra hits against the closest target within 12", more often as the game goes on.

**Sept Tenets.** If every unit is from the same sept (ignoring T'AU AUXILIARY, SUPREME COMMANDER and UNALIGNED units), your units gain that sept's tenet. The named septs are T'au Sept, Vior'la, Sa'cea, Dal'yth, Bork'an and Farsight Enclaves, or you can build your own.

**Battlesuits.** BATTLESUIT models can shoot while in Engagement Range, but only at the enemy units they are engaged with, and Heavy weapons take -1 to hit when they do.

**Manta Strike.** Units with this can start in reserve and arrive more than 9" from enemy models.

**Signature stratagems.** Saviour Protocols (1 CP) destroys a friendly <SEPT> DRONE within 3" (6" with a drone controller) to turn a failed save's Damage to 0. Combat Debarkation (1 CP) lets units disembark after their Devilfish makes a Normal Move. Strike and Fade (1 CP, or 2 CP for more than 5 non-DRONE models) lets a JET PACK unit shoot, then make a Normal Move of up to 6".

> **Tip:** The old For the Greater Good supporting-fire rule now appears only on a few Legends datasheets. Most T'au units need the Fire Overwatch Stratagem like everyone else.

## Key units

- [[Strike Team]] - Objective Secured Fire Warriors with long-ranged pulse rifles.
- [[Breacher Team]] - short-ranged, hard-hitting Fire Warriors who ride in Devilfish.
- [[Devilfish]] - tough transport; in Mont'ka it can move up to 9" before the first turn.
- [[Crisis Battlesuits]] - fast, flexible battlesuits with many weapon options and deep strike.
- [[Pathfinder Team]] - your best markerlight unit: it can start Fire Markerlights at the end of your Movement phase, after moving.
- [[Commander in Enforcer Battlesuit]] - a durable leader whose aura re-rolls hit rolls of 1 for nearby CORE units.
- [[Riptide Battlesuit]] - a Toughness 7 gun platform with a 4+ invulnerable save.
- [[Broadside Battlesuits]] - heavy firepower with a 2+ save that ignores the penalty for moving with Heavy weapons.
